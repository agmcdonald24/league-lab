Every number on this site is one of three things: a **projection** (what we expect a player to score), a **range**
(how far from that he could land) or a **record** (how those calls turned out). This post says what each one means,
how we check it, and where it has gone wrong.

## A projection is the middle, not a promise

**Projected points this week** is the average of the weeks a player like him, in his role, against this defense,
usually has — priced in the scoring you picked (Half PPR unless you open your league, and then your league's own
rules, bonus by bonus). We project the stat line first (targets, catches, yards, touchdowns, carries, passes) and
price it second, so the same projection reads differently in PPR and in Standard.

A projection of 14.2 does not mean "he will score 14". It means 14 is the middle of what usually happens.

## The range is the honest part

Beside most projections you will see a range:

- **Low-end / high-end outcome**: 1 week in 10 he lands below the low end, 1 in 10 above the high end. So 8 weeks in
  10 land inside it.
- **Typical range**: the middle half of outcomes. Half his weeks land inside it.

These ranges are wide on purpose. For a top-24 wide receiver in Half PPR the low-end to high-end span is about 17
points, and the typical range about 9. That is what a fantasy week is like; a narrower range would only look more
confident.

We check that the ranges mean what they say. Over three past seasons (2023–2025, every week, played games only):

| Position | Inside the typical range (aim 50%) | Inside low-end to high-end (aim 80%) |
|---|---|---|
| RB | 51.3% | about 80% |
| WR | 49.6% | about 80% |
| TE | 50.7% | about 80% |
| QB | 47.2% | 75.8% |

> Quarterbacks fall below the low end too often: about 15 weeks in 100 instead of 10. Almost all of those are games
> he did not finish (an injury, a benching). The model cannot see those coming, and we have not fixed it.

## How the record is kept

Every week, before the first kickoff, we save two boards side by side: **our projections and Sleeper's own**. After
the last game we score both against what happened, in the same scoring:

1. **Start/sit calls**: for each lineup, the closest calls between two players. Whose numbers picked the one who
   scored more?
2. **The order**: did the players we ranked higher score more?
3. **The miss**: the average distance between the projection and the points, per player.

Nothing is filled in after the fact. A week that was not saved before kickoff is not on the record, so the record
starts with the first week both boards were saved. It is kept in Half PPR, the scoring of the league we project every
morning. **About the numbers** shows it week by week, including the weeks we lost.

## Where we have been wrong

Through the first three complete weeks of 2026, against the same model's numbers on 2021–2025:

- **Quarterbacks are the weak spot.** The order of our quarterback projections matched the results much less well than
  in past seasons (0.39 this season against 0.59 before, where 1 is a perfect order and 0 is no better than a guess),
  and the average miss was 6.5 points against 5.4.
- **Running backs and wide receivers are on their usual form**: an average miss of 4.2 points per player for both,
  close to past seasons.
- **Tight ends miss a little more than usual**: 3.5 points against 3.0.
- **We sit a little below the market.** Our numbers for the top 24 players run about 1 to 3 points under Sleeper's
  at most positions. Part of that is a real difference of opinion; part is that the fringe players get a little too
  much. Read the gap as "we are a bit more cautious", not as a fade.
- **Our lineups' early record is small and unflattering**: on the first two rebuilt weeks of our own league, the
  lineups we would have set scored about 1.2 points less per team per week than the ones the managers set. Two weeks
  is far too few to judge, and the real record starts with lineups saved before kickoff.

## What none of these numbers know

A projection counts a player's role, his offense, the defense he faces and the betting lines. It does not know who
will be inactive on Sunday morning until the injury report says so, it does not know that a coach has decided to
change the plan, and a matchup note (a shutdown cornerback, a defense that has struggled) is context we show beside
the number, not something added to it.

If a number here looks wrong to you, it might be. Open the player and read **Why this number**: every projection
says what it is built from.
